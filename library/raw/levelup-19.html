Started as teenagers

Ben Pasternak
Apps · Sydney · age 15
Bored in science class at a Sydney high school, he made an iPhone game, Impossible Rush, with a friend in Chicago. It went to #1 in the Australian App Store. He then founded Flogg (a buy-and-sell app for friends) and Monkey (a video chat app for teens), which he later sold.
What this means for you: a 15-year-old in Sydney with a laptop can build something people use. With AI tools it's easier now than it was for him.
SMH (Oct 2014, Apr 2016); 9News; WAtoday (Jan 2018)
Benjamin "Kickz" Kapelushnik
Sneaker resale · Miami · age 13–16
Got into sneakers at 13 and resold his first pair (a gift from his mum) for almost double. By 16 he was the "Sneaker Don", supplying DJ Khaled, Drake and others, with nearly US$1 million in sales. Business Insider profiled him at 18.
What this means for you: knowing your scene better than anyone is a business skill.
Business Insider (Jan 2018); CNBC (Sep 2016); Complex; Wikipedia
Moziah "Mo" Bridges (Mo's Bows)
Accessories · Memphis · age 9
His grandma taught him to sew bow ties at 9. At 12 he pitched on Shark Tank. He didn't get a deal, but Daymond John became his mentor. At 15 he signed a seven-figure licensing deal with the NBA.
What this means for you: a "no" can still open a door if you keep going.
CBS News; NPR (Jul 2017); Business Insider (Jul 2017); TIME
Simon Beard (Culture Kings)
Streetwear · Gold Coast · teenager
Simon was a teenager when he started at the Carrara Markets. He said he always wanted to be an entrepreneur and "never work a real job". The full story is in the brand stories.
What this means for you: he started at your stage. A stall and a dream.
news.com.au

What they all have in common

They started with something they personally cared about.
They started small and cheap: markets, bedrooms, backyards, one shop.
They used the newest tools of their time: eBay, Facebook, Instagram, app stores. Yours is AI.
None of them had it all figured out at 15. They just started.
