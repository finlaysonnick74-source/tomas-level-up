Halfbrick (Fruit Ninja)
Gaming · Brisbane, 2001
A small group of young gamers fresh out of college started Halfbrick in the front bedroom of Shainiel Deo's house to make games they wanted to play. Their "Halfbrick Fridays" pitch sessions produced Fruit Ninja (2010), which passed 1 billion downloads.
For you: build the game or app you want. Test ideas fast with friends.
ABC Good Game (2011); Halfbrick submission to Parliament (aph.gov.au); SmartCompany (2010)
Sweat (Kayla Itsines)
Fitness app · Adelaide, 2014–15
Kayla Itsines started training women one-on-one in her parents' backyard, then in a local park. She turned it into an e-book, then the Sweat app (2015). In 2021 US company iFIT bought Sweat. Terms weren't disclosed; reports put it at about US$300–400 million.
For you: start face to face, then turn what works into an app.
Business Wire (iFIT, Jul 2021); WSJ; Business News Australia
Boost Juice
Food & drink · Adelaide, 2000
Janine Allis had no retail experience when she opened one juice bar in Adelaide's Rundle Mall. She picked Adelaide because a smaller market was easier to test in. The landlord kept the "For lease" sign handy, sure it would fail. Within four years Boost had 100 outlets across every state.
For you: test small, in one place, before going big.
BBC (2015); The Age (2011); Marketing Mag
Afterpay
Fintech · Sydney, 2014
At uni, Nick Molnar sold jewellery on eBay from his bedroom and became Australia's largest jewellery seller there. Selling online showed him how people wanted to pay. He co-founded Afterpay with Anthony Eisen in 2014. In 2021 Square (now Block) agreed to buy it for about A$39 billion.
For you: a small side hustle can show you a much bigger problem to solve.
Uni of Sydney (2018); Square press release (Aug 2021); Block (Jan 2022)
Rip Curl
Surf · Torquay, 1969
Surfers Doug "Claw" Warbrick and Brian "Sing Ding" Singer shaped surfboards in a garage, moved into an old bakery, then started making wetsuits. It became one of the world's biggest surf brands.
For you: build for your own crew first.
ripcurl.com (History); Torquay Museum Without Walls
Sneaker & streetwear corner
Small Aussie shops that lasted
ShoeGrab (Sydney, 2013): grew on social media with hand delivery, now calls itself Australia's largest sneaker marketplace. Up There (Melbourne, 2010): three young guys moved from Queensland and opened up a flight of stairs. Highs and Lows (Perth, 2005): started as a skate shop and had to petition customers just to get a Nike account. Sneakerboy (Melbourne, 2013): tiny stores and an app-based model.
