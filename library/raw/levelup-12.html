7Build: how to spot a good business

Some ideas burn bright for one summer. Others keep growing for decades.

Fad

One viral product that everyone suddenly wants, then nobody does.

Example: fidget spinners (2017). Huge for a few months, then shops were left with boxes of stock nobody would buy.

  • Easy to copy, so prices crash
  • Customers buy once
  • You're always chasing the next hype

Long-lasting

Meets a need people keep having, like clothes, transport, fixing things or saving time.

Example: surfwear. Billabong and Rip Curl started in the late '60s and early '70s, and people still buy boardshorts every summer.

  • Products can change, but the need stays
  • Customers come back
  • It can grow beyond just you
Tip: a fad isn't always bad. You can ride a trend for a bit of cash, as long as the business underneath (your brand, website, customers) can move on to the next thing.

The 6-question check

#QuestionWhy it matters
1Do I actually care about it?You'll stick with it when it gets hard or boring.
2Will people still want this in 5 years?That's the fad test.
3Can it grow beyond me?Could other people, tools or a website do some of the work?
4Will customers come back?Repeat customers are much cheaper than finding new ones.
5Can a website or app help it grow?This is where your AI coding skills become a superpower.
6Is there a decent profit per sale?Selling lots of things for $1 profit is exhausting.
