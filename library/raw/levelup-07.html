Why school matters to a business owner

Here's the honest pitch: school is free training for running a business. Every subject maps to something you'd otherwise pay for, or learn the hard way by losing money.

At schoolIn your businessA real example
MathsMargins, pricing, profit, stock you can affordBuy a polo for $9, sell it for $45, pay $6 shipping and fees. Is that a good deal? (Yes: about $30 profit.)
EnglishSelling. Product descriptions, emails, captions, pitching"Rare 90s Nike shorts, reworked by hand in Sydney" sells. "nike shorts good condition" doesn't.

Business studies, commerce, designThe literal subjectSome schools let you use your own business for assignments. Worth asking.

Turning up on time = turning up for a customer

If you said you'd deliver at 9 and you rock up at 9:40, they don't come back. School at 8:30 is the same muscle. Every on-time morning is a rep.

It keeps doors open

Your marks, attendance and a clean record are what banks, landlords, partners and big clients quietly check later. You don't have to love school. You just have to finish it with your options open.
Founder trick: treat the school bell like a customer meeting. Set your own alarm and get yourself up (that's your job, nobody else's), pack the night before, aim to be there five minutes early. Track it on your scorecard (section 8) and it starts unlocking business boosts.
Founders don't skip the boring bits. They just know why they're doing them.
