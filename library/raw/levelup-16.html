PILLAR 5 · BUILD · STORIES

Aussie brands that started tiny

Every one of these started with a few people, not much money, and something they cared about.

FEATURED STORY
Culture Kings
Streetwear & sneakers · Gold Coast, Queensland · 2008

Simon Beard was still a teenager when he and Tah-nee (now his wife) took a gamble on a market stall at the Carrara Markets on the Gold Coast. Simon had already been reselling shoes and hats online under the Culture Kings name. By the next year they'd scraped together enough to open their first store in Southport, leased month to month because they couldn't commit to more.

They made shopping feel like an event, with DJs and games in store. By 2020 the business was turning over almost $200 million. In 2021 the US group a.k.a. Brands bought it for $307 million cash plus shares, a deal reported as worth more than $600 million. Simon and Tah-nee stayed on as CEO and COO.

What this means for you: they started out reselling, just like you could, with a stall and a lot of hustle. The big difference came from making it their own: the experience, the vibe, the community.
Sources: news.com.au ("Just went nuts"); Business Wire, 31 Mar 2021 (a.k.a. Brands acquisition); Queensland Government media statement; Kalkine. Note: Business Wire says "founded in Brisbane"; most reports say Gold Coast.
Canva
Design tech · Perth, 2007 → 2013
At 19, Melanie Perkins saw uni students struggle with design software. With Cliff Obrecht she first built Fusion Books, an online yearbook designer, run from her mum's living room. It became Australia's largest yearbook publisher. Canva launched in 2013 after more than 100 investors said no.
For you: start with a small version of the big idea. Hearing "no" is normal.
BBC (Jan 2018); CNBC (Jan 2020); Wikipedia
Atlassian
Software · Sydney, 2002
Mike Cannon-Brookes and Scott Farquhar were 22 and at UNSW when they started with about $10,000 on credit cards. For three years they lived on about $300 a week of scholarship money and put everything back in. Atlassian listed on the US stock exchange in 2015.
For you: if customers pay you from day one, you don't need investors.
ABC News (Dec 2015); SMH (Jul 2010); SmartCompany
