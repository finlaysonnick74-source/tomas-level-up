Tools I'll master

Fourteen real tools.

The same tools professionals use, each learned on the day I need it.

Grok Bot AI builder

An AI assistant that writes and changes code when you describe what you want.

You'll use it for: Building every page, fixing bugs, and explaining code when you ask “why?”.

Learned on: Day 1, Day 2, Day 3, Day 4, Day 6, Day 8, Day 9, Day 10, Day 11, Day 12, Day 14, Day 15, Day 17, Day 19

GitHub Stores code

A website that keeps your code safe online, with every version saved.

You'll use it for: Daily commits, branches, pull requests, issues (to-dos) and the v1.0 release.

Learned on: Day 1, Day 2, Day 3, Day 4, Day 5, Day 6, Day 7, Day 8, Day 9, Day 10, Day 11, Day 12, Day 15, Day 17, Day 19, Day 20, Day 21

Netlify Hosting

A host that puts your site online and updates it every time you push to GitHub.

You'll use it for: Test address, preview links, the contact form, rollbacks and the live site.

Learned on: Day 2, Day 3, Day 7, Day 8, Day 10, Day 12, Day 16, Day 19

Canva Graphics

An easy design app with templates for logos, images and social posts.

You'll use it for: Your logo and brand kit, website images, highlight covers and Instagram posts.

Learned on: Day 2, Day 3, Day 5, Day 6, Day 9, Day 13, Day 14, Day 18, Day 20, Day 21

Mobbin Inspiration

A huge library of screenshots from real, well-designed apps and websites.

You'll use it for: Collecting ideas for your homepage, pricing page and contact form.

Learned on: Day 3, Day 4

Figma Optional

A design tool pros use to lay out pages before they are built.

You'll use it for: Optional: turning your paper wireframes into neat digital ones.

Learned on: Day 3, Day 5

Browser DevTools Inspect

Tools built into Chrome (right-click → Inspect) that show the code behind any page.

You'll use it for: Studying other sites, phone-size testing, and Lighthouse speed and quality checks.

Learned on: Day 3, Day 4, Day 6, Day 11, Day 14, Day 15

Squoosh Image shrinker

A free Google web app that makes image files smaller without looking worse.

You'll use it for: Shrinking every photo and graphic so your pages load fast on phones.

Learned on: Day 3, Day 6, Day 9

Domain registrar & DNS Web address

The shop you rent your web address from, and the settings that point it at your site.

You'll use it for: Buying your domain with Dad and pointing it at Netlify (with HTTPS).

Learned on: Day 16

Google Search Console Get found

A free Google tool that shows whether Google has found your pages and how people search for you.

You'll use it for: Verifying your site and sending Google your sitemap.

Learned on: Day 17

Google Analytics Measure

A free tool that counts visits: how many, from where, and which pages.

You'll use it for: Watching real visitors arrive and seeing which came from Instagram.

Learned on: Day 17, Day 19, Day 21

Google Business Profile Local search

Your free listing on Google Search and Maps, with services, reviews and a website link.

You'll use it for: Showing up when locals search “web design Northern Beaches”.

Learned on: Day 18

Instagram (business) Be seen

A business account on Instagram, with Insights: numbers on who saw and tapped your posts.

You'll use it for: Your shop window: bio, link to your site, posts, and checking Insights.

Learned on: Day 13, Day 19, Day 20, Day 21

Postiz Scheduling

A scheduler: plan a batch of posts and it publishes them on time for you.

You'll use it for: Scheduling your launch-week posts, including the launch post on Day 21.

Learned on: Day 2, Day 20, Day 21

Cursor Code editor

A code editor with AI built in, where you open your project, read the code and make changes.

You'll use it for: Opening your site on your own computer, editing with AI help and committing to GitHub.

Learned on: Day 3
